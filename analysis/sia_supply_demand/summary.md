# SUS 102/103 under Core Education: what the enrollment store says

**Prepared:** 2026-10-07. **Measure:** enrollment per section at the snapshot closest to day 20 after classes_begin, with quality tags per section (see data_load.py). **Scope:** Summer 2024 through Fall 2026 for SUS (combined legacy registrar table and live tracker); Fall 2025 through Fall 2026 for the full SIA (CFSI) category (live tracker only).

## Requirement change: context for everything below

Baccalaureate Core (admitted before Summer 2025) required three natural-science Perspectives courses: one Biological Science, one Physical Science, and one Additional, with no more than two in the same department. Core Education (admitted Summer 2025 and after, term 202600+) requires only two natural-science SIA courses with lab, and they must come from two different subject codes. So the Core Ed cohort faces (i) one fewer required science course, and (ii) a strict one-per-subject cap. See [OSU Core Ed SIA page](https://catalog.oregonstate.edu/core-education/scientific-inquiry-analysis/).

Two other dynamics matter when reading the SUS 102/103 numbers. First, we have prior registrar evidence that roughly 10 to 15 percent of pre-Core-Ed students took BOTH SUS 102 and SUS 103, meaning under the new one-per-subject rule those students must now pick one. Second, SIA includes many courses that students also take to satisfy their own major requirements (BI 221Z and CH 121 are obvious examples). Students in science-adjacent majors effectively "double-count" one SIA course against both their major and Core Ed. Courses that are heavily required by majors therefore shrink less than the mechanical requirement change would predict, while elective SIA courses (plausibly including SUS 102 and SUS 103) shrink more.

## Headline findings for SUS 102/103

**1. Combined 102+103 enrollments are falling about as fast as the mechanical change predicts.** Fall totals (102 and 103 together): 1,408 in FA24, 1,174 in FA25, 822 in FA26 (day 13). Winter and Spring are smaller drops (WI25 to WI26: 1,238 to 1,169, down 6 percent; SP25 to SP26: 1,287 to 1,145, down 11 percent). Using the 10-15 percent double-taker figure, the forced single-count rule alone subtracts roughly 8-12 percent from the combined. The 3-to-2 course reduction removes about a third of each Core Ed student's science demand, applied against a Core Ed cohort share that grows from zero in FA24 to perhaps one third in FA25 to perhaps two thirds in FA26. Those two mechanical effects together are roughly consistent with the observed FA24-to-FA25 drop (17 percent) and sit at the lower edge of the FA25-to-FA26 drop (30 percent). The data are therefore consistent with a mostly-mechanical story. They do not require, and do not strongly show, a loss of student interest in SUS.

**2. The pattern within 102 vs 103 does not look like clean within-SUS reassignment.** FA25 to FA26: SUS 102 fell 34 percent and SUS 103 fell 23 percent. In Winter 2026, 103 ran slightly above WI25 while 102 fell. If the only thing happening were double-takers forced to pick, one of 102 or 103 would absorb the picks and sit above its old level while the other dropped; instead both fall in Fall, which suggests Fall students who used to take only one of 102 or 103 are now also leaving the pair (consistent with mechanical reduction plus some displacement to a major-fulfilling course).

**3. The courses that grew in SIA are the ones that double as major prerequisites.** SIA seats expanded 14 percent and enrollment 13 percent FA25 to FA26, with fill rate near 92 percent. Biggest gainers: BI (+34 percent, driven by BI 221Z nearly doubling, 2,396 to 3,749), CH (+32 percent, mostly CH 121, 1,548 to 2,041), BOT (+29 percent), PH (+8 percent). These are the standard STEM major prerequisites that students were going to take anyway and that now also tick the Core Ed box. Their growth is almost certainly driven by major-track enrollment (and department-side section expansion to meet it, not by Core Ed students newly choosing these fields. The implication for SUS is that any Core Ed student in a science-adjacent major will take BI 221Z or CH 121 (because they have to for the major) and will then only need one more SIA course from a different subject code. That remaining slot used to be a free pick where SUS could compete; now it is one of two slots where double-counting with the major has already taken the easy one.

**4. SUS looks like GEO in the pattern of losses, but that's an observation, not a mechanism.** Of thirteen multi-course SIA subjects, SUS (-30 percent) and GEO (-25 percent) are the only big double-digit losers; the rest grew or were flat. SUS and GEO share a plausible profile: both are "service" subjects for Core Ed with limited presence as major prerequisites. This is consistent with the mechanism in finding 3 and does not require any special story about SUS specifically being disfavored. SUS seats fell 20 percent (program-side reduction) while enrollment fell 30 percent; GEO seats fell only 10 percent while enrollment fell 25 percent, meaning GEO is leaving more empty seats on the table. SUS has already begun to right-size supply to demand.

**5. Supply diversity is roughly stable but capacity is concentrating in the giants.** Distinct SIA courses offered rose from 32 in FA25 to 34 in FA26, across 18 and then 19 subject codes. Median seats per course was essentially unchanged (217 in FA25, 210 in FA26). The top five courses, however, took a larger share of SIA capacity: 57 percent in FA25, 61 percent in FA26. Those top five are always the standard STEM-major prerequisites (BI 221Z, PH 201, CH 121, PH 211, BI 101). The remaining long tail is 27 to 29 courses with a median of ~170 to 190 seats each, which is a healthy menu by numbers; it is just not where new seats are being created. In Winter and Spring the top-five list usually includes SUS 102 or 103 (there are fewer giants in off-Fall terms), which is why the Winter and Spring SUS declines are shallower. In Fall, SUS is firmly in the tail and competes against ~30 other options that are not expanding either.

## What this data can and cannot say

CAN say: enrollment levels by term, campus, and section; direction and magnitude of change; subject-level share within SIA; which SIA courses were offered which term.

CANNOT say: whether a given 102 enrollee also took 103; whether an enrollee is on Bacc Core or Core Ed; what their major is; whether they chose SUS freely or defaulted to it because their major did not supply a free SIA course; waitlist counts (not stored). I cannot therefore separate the mechanical requirement reduction, the within-SUS forced-choice effect, the major-fulfilling-SIA displacement effect, and the free-preference-against-SUS effect. The registrar requests below are what would let us untangle those.

## Data artefacts

The daily tracker closes its window about six days after classes begin, so for every term since Winter 2026 the available census per section is roughly day 6, not day 20. Fall 2025 happens to have late-term snapshots from an earlier tracker configuration, and the SUS legacy table reaches further into older terms. Fall 2026 numbers come from the last snapshot before the analysis freeze, 2026-10-06, which is day 13 of the term (classes began 2026-09-23). *Corrected 2026-11-23: an earlier version of this note gave the start as 2026-09-30 and the snapshot as day 7, inherited from a term-date bug since fixed; the affected figures move by under one percent.* Enrollment is flat across that window, 813 at day 7 against 809 at day 13, so the Fall 2026 total is at or just past peak rather than still climbing. Cross-term comparisons mix census quality; late-term FA25 values are slightly below peak enrollment (mid-term drops), while day-6 to day-13 values are at or near peak. The net direction of this bias slightly understates the FA25-to-FA26 decline, so the headline is conservative.

A separate artefact: a few SIA courses appear and disappear from the category snapshot between adjacent terms in ways that look like data-collection changes rather than catalog edits (BI 101, BI 221Z, ENSC 210, PH 201 among them). Entries and exits are flagged in C4_course_entries_exits.csv for review before quoting subject-level growth.

## Registrar requests (not possible from this store)

- Share of past SUS 102 students who also took SUS 103 under Bacc Core, by cohort year. User's prior work put this near 10-15 percent; a current update would tighten the mechanical calculation.
- Among currently enrolled SUS 102 and 103 students, counts of Core Ed versus Bacc Core, by section.
- Majors (or last-known interest) of current SUS 102 and 103 students, cross-tabulated with majors of the counterfactual SIA cohort (BI 221Z, CH 121, PH 201 enrollees). This is what identifies whether SUS is losing students who had SUS-aligned interests, or whether SUS is simply losing the share of students whose majors do not auto-fulfill SIA.
